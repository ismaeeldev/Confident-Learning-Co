Mock results land in a particular way in most households: as data. A number, a grade boundary, a sense of where things stand with the real exams not far off. For a lot of teenagers, though, a low mock does not land as data. It lands as confirmation — of something they quietly decided about themselves a long time before this particular exam, often years before.

## The result is rarely the real story

If a teenager has spent Years 7 to 9 gradually disengaging from a subject — not revising properly, not asking for help when they got stuck, coasting through in a way that avoided ever really testing themselves — a mock result at sixteen is not measuring their ceiling. It is measuring three years of protective distance from the subject, compressed into two hours and a grade boundary. But it rarely feels that way in the moment. In the moment, it feels like proof.

This matters because how a teenager reads their mock result determines what they do next. A teenager who reads a low mock as "I am not clever enough" usually responds by trying even less before the real exam, because trying and getting the same result again would confirm it further, and not trying keeps the door open to "I could have done better." A teenager who reads the same result as "I have not properly engaged with this yet" responds completely differently — because the fix is available to them, rather than a verdict on who they are.

## Why "you'll be fine, don't worry about it" doesn't help

This reassurance skips past the actual problem. Your teenager is not primarily worried about the number on the page. They are worried about what the number means about them. Telling them not to worry about a number they've already stopped focusing on does not touch the belief sitting underneath it.

## What actually helps

Talk about the result as information about the last two years, not as information about them. Something like: "That mock is telling us about how the last couple of years of this subject went, not about how capable you are. What would eight weeks of properly engaging with it actually look like?" This reframes the conversation from verdict to plan, which is a much easier place for a teenager to stand.

It also helps to separate the mock from the real exam explicitly and repeatedly. A mock exists to reveal gaps while there is still time to close them. Say that plainly, more than once, because most teenagers have absorbed the opposite message from the way mocks are talked about at school.

## It is later, not too late

By this stage there is a countdown behind almost every conversation about school, which can make even a calm, supportive approach feel like pressure to a teenager who is already braced for it. The method that would have worked at thirteen needs adjusting for someone this age — not abandoning, just fitted to the young person actually in front of you, with the time that is actually left.
